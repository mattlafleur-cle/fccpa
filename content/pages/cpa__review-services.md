## What a review actually provides

A financial statement review is an independent CPA engagement under AICPA standards (SSARS). It is not bookkeeping, and it is not an audit.

### Limited assurance.

The CPA report states that the CPA is not aware of any material modifications that should be made for the statements to conform with the applicable financial reporting framework (often GAAP).

### Independence.

The CPA must be independent of the organization.

### Procedures.

Work centers on inquiries of management and analytical procedures applied to the financial statements. It does not include testing internal controls the way an audit does, or verifying balances with third parties as a matter of course.

### Your responsibility.

Management remains responsible for the financial statements and for the underlying books and records.

### What is the difference between a review and an audit?

An audit provides reasonable assurance and includes testing of evidence and consideration of internal control. A review provides limited assurance and is based mainly on inquiries and analytical procedures. If your lender or regulator specifically requires an audit, a review will not substitute. We can help you read the requirement and point you in the right direction.

### Will my bank accept a review?

Many lenders accept reviewed financial statements when that is what the covenant or commitment letter asks for. Bring the exact language. We confirm whether a review meets the request before we start. We do not guarantee that every bank will accept a review.

### Do my books need to be perfect first?

They need to be complete enough for a review. If they are not, we will say so and can discuss cleanup before the review begins.

### Do you review both businesses and nonprofits?

Yes. The reporting framework can differ, but the need is the same: an independent CPA report your stakeholders can rely on at the review level.
