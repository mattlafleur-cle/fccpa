## Three problems we see constantly

These are common, fixable, and worth catching early.

### Ministers paid as contractors.

Ministers are generally common-law employees for income tax purposes and should be on payroll. Getting this wrong creates penalties and puts the housing allowance at risk.

### Housing allowance designated after the fact.

It has to be designated in advance, in writing, by the governing body. Retroactive designation does not work and it costs the minister real money.

### Restricted funds spent on operations.

A gift given for one purpose and spent on another is a legal and donor-trust problem, not just a bookkeeping one. It needs to be tracked in the system, not in someone's memory.
